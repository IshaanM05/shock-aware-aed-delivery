The **walking route factor** (median 1.54) is what the rover model uses to turn a straight-line response radius into a route length.

## What OpenStreetMap does not tell us here (coverage caveat)

* Only 1.4% of road length carries a sidewalk tag and 98.2% is untagged for sidewalks; dedicated pedestrian ways are 5.1% of the walking network.
* The area contains only 24 tagged crossing nodes and 1 kerb-tagged node (77% of sampled routes contain zero tagged crossings).
* Tagged crossings per km of walking route: pooled 0.24. A geometric fallback that counts transversal crossings of primary, secondary and tertiary roads gives pooled 0.18 per km. Both are **lower bounds**: real streets in this area have far more crossings than the map records.

## How this feeds the model

* The walking route factor is used as measured.
* Crossing density is **not** taken from the map. The clinical analysis sweeps crossings per km over the OSM-tagged lower bound and assumed urban values (2, 4 and 8 per km) and reports every one.
* The driving route factor is reported for context only: the ambulance response-time distribution comes from a published study (Naess et al. 2024), not from this network.
